The problem on most on-chain sales is not the transfer. It is the answer. A seller lists work. A buyer pays. Then someone — a company, a Discord admin, a screenshot — has to say the money landed. Other contracts cannot ask that company. Agents cannot either. They either get the payload for free, or they poll a private API that can lie.

Paidline is a public marketplace with a payment checker. You list work on Creditcoin. Anyone can buy it. Buyers send exact USDC on Ethereum. Paidline never holds the dollars. Attestcoin proves that transfer. A Creditcoin contract is the only thing that may say it is paid. First matching payment claims the listing and unlocks the work.

This is the live board. Looking is free. You only connect a wallet to list or to pay. Listing nine is already paid — a real ten USDC transfer on Sepolia, stamped on Creditcoin. The cards on the right are still open. Anyone can buy them.

Four steps. List on Creditcoin. Pay on Ethereum. Prove with Attestcoin. Confirm with isPaid. Paidline is a checker, not a custodian. USDC never moves to Creditcoin. Only the fact of payment does.

The marketplace is every funded unpaid listing. Eight are open right now. Each has a USDC price, a small Creditcoin receipt bond, and work sealed at list time. Two listings cannot share the same chain, token, destination, and amount. So one payment can only mean one invoice.

To publish, you name the work, set a USDC price, attach the brief, and lock a little tCTC as a receipt bond. The brief never goes on-chain. Buyers see it only after the contract says paid. Checkout asks for a few micro-USDC above the sticker, so two retainers at the same price can sit on the same wallet without colliding.
